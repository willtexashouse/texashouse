Becoming a company

Texas House started as an event. It is becoming a company. Everything in motion right now is one of three jobs: Part I builds the board that raises the money and holds us to the standard. Part II settles what we sell, who buys it, and what they get. Part III looks past the activations at the lines of business they could become. The first two meet in the middle — the board brief answers the positioning session's first question.

The board of a private, for-profit corporation. Directors owe their duty to the shareholders, hold equity, and are elected each year by the controlling shareholder. Three jobs:

RaiseBring in sponsors, partners, and capital. Every seat maps to a source of money, or a source of legitimacy that leads to money.

Equity is the engine. Directors raise money because they own part of the outcome — the vesting cliff and the annual election tie the reward to the work.

§04

Terms for directors

add feedback
TermOne year, elected annually by shareholders. Re-election follows the annual review by the governance and nominating committee.
CompensationRestricted common stock at seating. No cash; reasonable expenses reimbursed. Grant size set per seat, informed by the governance chair.
Vesting12-month cliff — nothing vests before the first anniversary; then monthly over 36 months. Leave before the cliff, hold nothing. 83(b) filed within 30 days of grant; the company provides the form and deadline.
ExpectationsFour quarterly meetings, in person where possible. Written consents inside five business days. A written year-one expectation tied to the seat spec. Conflicts disclosed, recusal where appropriate.
ProtectionD&O insurance before the first meeting. Indemnification in the bylaws. A director agreement covering term, grant, expectations, and exit.

Why the cliff instead of a probation period: a grant at seating with a 12-month cliff does the same job and is the structure experienced directors expect. The company should be worth more in a year — waiting to grant makes the stock a larger taxable event. A director with a grant on paper is committed in a way a director with a promise is not.

§06

How seats get filled

add feedback

The board recruits the board, starting with seat five: specification first — candidates are measured against the spec, not against who is available. The committee owns the search. Every seated director puts forward three names from their own network. Warm approach, one conversation with Will, then a written offer — seat, term, grant, vesting, year-one expectation, D&O — before anyone says yes. Committee recommends, board approves, shareholders elect.

What we are not doing: filling a seat to fill it — an open seat with a live search reads as deliberate, a weak director does not. Recruiting from the founder's immediate circle — the people who can raise money for Texas House are mostly people we have not met yet. Promising equity before the spec, the expectation, and the paperwork are set.

Part II: What we sell

This part is not for writing a tagline. It is for agreeing on what we sell, who buys it, and what they get. The tagline falls out of that in about ten minutes once the rest is settled.

Every line we run in market today is about Texas. Not one is about the buyer. A sponsor hears all of them and still has no idea what they are purchasing — which is why every meeting turns into "so how do you make money, and what are we paying for?" That question is not skepticism. It is the buyer trying to do our job for us.

§19

The flagship house

add feedback

Two private clubs already prove the physical model in Texas. What they built, what they charge, and what each one says about the room it wants.

They come from opposite directions. Park House is the Texas-born version: founded in Dallas by hoteliers who had lived inside London's clubs, opened in Highland Park Village in 2018, and grown to a second house in Houston. Soho House is the global one: a London chain of forty-eight Houses in nineteen countries whose first Texas house landed on South Congress in 2021. One shows what a club built here can charge; the other shows that the international member will come to Austin for it.

Park House · Dallas and Houston

The London club, translated to Texas.

Opened 2018 in Highland Park Village. Founded by John and Deborah Scott (John ran Rosewood and Belmond) with Megan and Brady Wood (WoodHouse). Modelled on London's private clubs, 5 Hertford Street and Annabel's among them, and positioned as the alternative to the country club and the business club. Three dining rooms, private dining, library and living rooms, a gallery with a rotating art programme, screenings, live performance, tastings. Houston opened December 2023: 25,000 square feet at 4411 San Felipe.
The money: Resident members (30+) pay a $7,500 initiation and $292 a month; Junior (23 to 30) $2,800 and $146; non-resident $4,800 and $146. Founding members paid $25,000 with dues waived for life. A capped Culture membership runs $500 a year.

Sources: parkhousedallas.com/membership · PaperCity, 2023

Soho House · Austin

A global brand's first Texas room.

Opened summer 2021 at 1011 South Congress in the Music Lane development, the first House in Texas. Forty-six bedrooms across two floors, a rooftop pool and bar, a screening room, Club Cecconi's, Dante's HiFi bar, a gym partnership. Interiors built with around seventy local artisans and Texas designers, in what the company calls Texas Modernism. Membership is by application: a photo, an occupation, your interests.
The money: Local House membership in Austin is quoted from $937.50 a quarter; press reports put the annual figure between roughly $2,650 and $3,750. Every House, all forty-eight Houses in nineteen countries, is $4,300 a year. Under-27 pricing runs $475 a quarter. Soho Friends, the entry product, is $130 a year.

Sources: sohohouse.com · Austin · membership · Austin American-Statesman, 2021

What the numbers say

The buyers paid about 2.2 times revenue and 20 times EBITDA for Soho House, and they paid it for membership fees: the one line that grew 17% while food and rooms stood still. Membership is the asset. Park House shows the other end of the scale: a two-house Texas club is a business in the low tens of millions, built on about $14 million of outside money and a $25,000 founding-member device. That is the size of thing a Texas House flagship would be, and the Soho multiple is what a membership book is worth if it keeps growing.

What the two models have in common, and what we would do differently

Both sell the same thing: a room that is hard to get into, programmed so there is a reason to come back, with food and drink carrying the daily economics and initiation fees carrying the capital. Both lead with culture, not business, and let business happen. Both price the individual around three to four thousand dollars a year, plus an entry fee at Park House.

The Texas House difference is the calendar. Neither club has a moment the whole world attends. We do, three or four times a year, and a house is what makes those moments a year-round relationship instead of a week. Park House proves a Texas-founded club can charge London prices; Soho House proves the global buyer will come to South Congress. A Texas House flagship sits between them: the place a delegation lands, a founder brings a counterpart, a partner hosts, on the weeks that matter and every week between.

A room of their own.

Questions this raises for Part I and Part II
  • The entity question in §10 gets sharper: memberships and a founding-member raise are a for-profit's tools. The board brief already assumes the C-corp; this is why it matters.
  • Two products for one buyer is a pricing problem. A consulate that is a corporate member should not also be sold a presenting partnership at full rate; the ladder needs a rule for that, the same way in-kind needed one in §14.
  • A house is a real-estate and hospitality business before it is a network. It needs an operator in the way Park House has the Scotts and Woods. Seat six or seat seven on the board may be that person.
  • Sequence: lane one can start this season inside the existing activations. Lane two needs the house, or at least the salons, to be real. The founding-member device only works once there is a floor plan to show.
