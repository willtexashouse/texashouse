## Organization

Texas House is a public affairs and innovation organization based in Austin,
Texas. Three things define the mission:

1. We are building the network of ascending leaders in Texas.
2. We tell the story of contemporary Texas.
3. We connect Texas to the world and the world to Texas.

The work happens through activations at major global moments (SXSW Austin,
SXSW London, Formula 1), curated connections between operators, and
superconnector programming across music, film, research, technology, policy,
and areas of economic opportunity.

## Audience

- **Primary — prospective partners and sponsors.** Brands, agencies, economic
  development organizations, venture firms, and institutions who want access to
  Texas at a moment of global attention.
- **Secondary — ascending leaders.** Founders, investors, creatives, and policy
  operators who want into the network.
- **Tertiary — press and public** tracking Texas innovation.

## Primary conversion

**Become a Partner.** Every page routes to it. The nav CTA and a closing CTA
band appear on every page.

## Tone and visual direction

Contemporary Texas, not kitsch Western. Confident, plainspoken, institutional
without being stiff. Editorial layout logic with a strong display typeface and
generous white space. Photography-forward using event imagery.

Recurring visual motifs:

- An anchored cowboy hat as a persistent brand object. In the hero, a cowboy hat
  resting on a slowly rotating globe.
- Cowboy-on-bull imagery as inset background texture behind content blocks, not
  full-bleed decoration.
- Postcard-style cards with slight rotation and stacking, for activations and cities.
- A horizontal scrolling brand marquee.

Neutral base palette with one saturated accent color. Wireframes should indicate
where the accent carries weight.

**Footer** — logo, one-line mission statement, three nav columns, social icons,

## Required amendment before generating

Version A as written says *"neutral base palette with one saturated accent
color"* and never names the Texas House brand. Pasted as-is, a design tool will
**invent its own palette and type**, which violates THE RULE in
`component-inventory.md`. Paste this block into the prompt under
"TONE AND VISUAL DIRECTION":

```
BRAND — NON-NEGOTIABLE. Use these exact values. Do not substitute or invent.

Ground        #181210 (gunmetal)      Deep ground   #0e0b09
Type          #faf6f0 (ivory)         Dimmed type   #e7decd
Accent        #793f34 (clay)          Accent bright #9a4d3e
Secondary     #b85a3a rust · #c7a87a dune · #606e51 sage · #3d4a76 bluebonnet

Display type  TAY Dreamboat — all caps, tracking -0.12em at hero scale,
              -0.05em below ~36px
Eyebrow caps  BN Bergen St — tracking 0.12em
Body          Lato
Mono          SF Mono / ui-monospace

Motion        140ms fast / 240ms base, cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.2, 1)

Signatures    Bracket tags — [LEAD], [OUTBOUND] — for categories and labels.
              Dashed clay rules as section separators.
```

## Open question this raises — NOW ANSWERABLE

> **Update 2026-08-19:** the CRM turned out to ship **both** themes. Every
> `--crm-*` token is defined twice — dark on `.crm-app`, light on
> `.crm-app[data-theme="light"]` — each with measured WCAG ratios. So a light
> Texas House site is **brand-sanctioned, not invented**. Both are now live in
> `src/styles/global.css` and rendered side by side at `/brand`.
> **Decide by looking at the specimen, not from the description below.**


The brief asks for *"generous white space"* and *"photography-forward"*, which
reads as a **light editorial** site. The CRM brand is a **dark system** —
gunmetal ground, ivory type. Both can't be true.

**This needs Will's call before wireframes are generated:**

- **(a) Dark**, matching the CRM exactly — gunmetal ground throughout, ivory
  type, clay accent. Maximum brand continuity with the internal platform.
- **(b) Light editorial** using the same palette inverted — ivory ground,
  gunmetal type, clay accent, same fonts and signatures. Still "conforms to the
  CRM" in type, accent, spacing, and motion, but flips the ground for a
  photography-forward public site.

Option (b) is the more conventional choice for a public, image-led marketing
site, and it keeps every CRM token — it just swaps which one is background and
which is foreground. Option (a) is the stricter reading of THE RULE.
